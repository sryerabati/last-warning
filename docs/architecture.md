# Architecture

## Local application

A local FastAPI service exposes the simulation, warning library, saved experiments, Bedrock agent decisions and TRIBE Mini endpoints. The production React build and `/api` share port 8000; Vite proxies the API during development. SQLite stores versioned JSON metadata; compressed recordings, stimuli and numerical artifacts live in the local data directory. Connections close after each transaction. Recordings are written using atomic replacement before their metadata is inserted.

Each completed run snapshots its scenario, warning, initial-condition hash, simulation version, agent decisions, provider usage, optional neural reference, frames and events. Replay and seeking read those saved records without invoking Bedrock or Mini again. Editing or deleting a library warning does not rewrite completed runs. Up to two simulations may run concurrently; an additional request receives HTTP 429.

## Current campus engine

New work starts in Ohio State's mapped academic core and neighborhood. Campus engine 3.0 uses `CampusLife`, `CampusTraffic`, `CampusTransit` and `CampusHazard` over pinned metric walking/driving graphs. Its response timing runs in physical seconds independently of output sampling; its Bedrock protocol is `nova_resident_json_campus_v3`. Explicit campus day types change routines and service windows. Cars/pedestrians coordinate crossing conflicts, buses carry actual queued passengers, and saved hazard contact episodes interrupt movement and drive fall/bracing/recovery poses. Additive recordings retain exact movement, transfer and vehicle-visibility transitions. No schema migration rewrites historical runs.

See [campus emergency architecture](campus-emergency-engine.md), [movement coordination](campus-mobility.md), and [calendar/transit mechanics](research/campus-calendar-transit.md) for current contracts and authored assumptions. Campus previews cache two complete day configurations, including day type and transit choice. The earlier organic/grid model below remains supported for historical recordings and explicit legacy API configurations.

## Legacy living town

The organic Riverside world’s winding river, connected lanes, bridges, footpaths, building footprints, entrances and refuges are canonical backend geometry, shared by routing, hazards and pixel rendering. Old grid recordings retain their own geometry. Scenario fields `layout`, `town_life` and `town_start_tick` are additive; omitted values keep the legacy API behavior. Existing metadata and recordings require no destructive migration.

`simulation/life.py` records one deterministic day locally: 288 frames at five minutes of town time per frame. Residents have seeded fictional names, households, homes, roles, work/school destinations, social connections and individual departure offsets. Their itineraries include sleep, home, work/school, shops, parks and civic visits. Journeys traverse actual graph edges and export their motion paths. Conversations are recorded between co-located people, with participant IDs and expiry; the app does not invent dialogue or imply a brain prediction describes their thoughts.

Indoor occupancy is a real named-place state. Its logical routing coordinate is the building entrance; interior rooms, injuries and building damage are not simulated. Indoor sprites hide, and selecting an occupied building selects an actual occupant. Day/night tint and lit windows present recorded time and occupancy rather than an independent visual clock. Pausing stops daily progress, walking and atmospheric animation.

`POST /api/town-life/preview` returns the complete day, canonical city and resident profiles without invoking Bedrock or TRIBE. A bounded four-world cache serves repeated previews. The HUD starts at 08:00, offers time jumps and speed controls, and loops through midnight after residents have returned home. Loading a saved emergency recording suspends daily playback; returning to town restores the live viewing position.

Running a warning freezes the visible daily tick into the scenario. The emergency model reconstructs those exact positions and routines, rather than spawning a fresh road crowd. Baseline comparisons reuse that frozen configuration. In living-town mode Bedrock receives actual household, home/work, activity and occupancy context, with an additional eligible `CONTINUE_ROUTINE` choice. Protocol `nova_resident_json_life_v1` distinguishes that action from `WAIT`, which explicitly holds position. Preparation, sharing and evacuation interrupt routine travel. Ambient schedules are local simulation mechanics; emergency choices and their reasons still come from the real model. Legacy experiments retain their four-action protocol.

## Resident decisions and simulation physics

`Simulation` subclasses Mesa 3.3.1 `Model`; each `Resident` subclasses Mesa `Agent`. Residents have individual trust, movement speed, social connections, seeded everyday priorities and decision memory. Priorities include work, family, errands, household routines and checking on neighbors; slower residents also account for their actual walking pace. Goals are saved in the initial conditions rather than instructing everyone to seek shelter from the outset. Their explicit states are unaware, alerted, deciding, preparing, evacuating, sheltered, stranded and exposed.

The UI's Bedrock mode delegates resident behavior to real Amazon Nova Micro inference through `backend/app/bedrock.py`. At ticks 4, 16, 32 and 56, the engine supplies a separate context for every eligible resident: actual position/state, goal, trust, mobility, received warning, visible local danger, accessible shelter route, neighbors and recent decisions. Residents without a delivered warning have no perceived warning text; without a warning or a local observation their hazard type is unknown. A prior local observation remains in their knowledge. The provider prompt treats warnings and other input text as data, and restricts decisions to each resident's available actions.

The provider batches 10 distinct resident contexts per plain Bedrock Converse request, with one in-flight provider request per service process, a 3,000-token output bound and a 60-second read timeout. Explicit SDK session/client creation and requests share a global semaphore; TLS certificate verification remains enabled. This compatibility guard was added after a native macOS OpenSSL certificate-validation crash. [AWS documents](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/boto3/latest/guide/clients.html) the risk of concurrent client creation through the shared default session. The guard reduces concurrency without changing resident contexts, prompts, decisions or budgets.

Protocol `nova_resident_json_v4` requests a compact JSON decision object. Its parser requires the complete requested resident ID set exactly once, an eligible action for each resident and a short nonempty reason; a complete round is validated before its actions are applied. One bounded retry is available for a transient request failure or an invalid response, with validation feedback supplied when a response needs correction. Each paid attempt is reserved and accounted independently. An exhausted retry stops the run; no model substitute or rule response is invented. Wait/preparation hold position; sharing creates actual delayed social messages; evacuation follows the current accessible road route. Model decisions bypass the rule mode's notice/action/share probability gates and preparation timer. Delivery reach, broadcast time, channel availability and message transit remain explicit simulation mechanisms.

Missing credentials, provider failures, spending limits and invalid/incomplete responses stop the run explicitly. There is no silent rule-based substitute. Model actions/reasons/ticks are recorded in events and frame inspector data; model ID, token use, request evidence and estimated/accounted costs accompany the run. A durable SQLite spending ledger reserves calls before sending them, with a $0.30 per-run cap, $5 Bedrock phase cap and $30 project ceiling. Uncertain calls retain their reservation. These are local accounting limits, not confirmation of the AWS billing dashboard.

NetworkX multi-source Dijkstra computes shortest accessible shelter paths whenever closures change. Mesa/NetworkX movement, congestion, exposure and shelter arrival remain authoritative regardless of the model's recommendation. Each resident moves only along currently accessible graph edges; the LLM cannot invent coordinates, clear a blocked road or grant safety. Tornado scenarios use additional nearby designated storm shelters; flood and wildfire use the original two protected refuges.

## Hazards and reproducibility

`world.py` generates a fictional 40 × 40 city, streets, river, bridges, terrain and shelters. `hazards.py` supplies cumulative affected cells, currently active danger, road closures and rendering metadata. Flood is terrain-constrained neighboring inundation with depth-triggered closures. Wildfire has wind/uphill-biased spread, a river fuel break and finite burning; burned roads remain closed. Tornado has a moving wind footprint and persistent debris closures. These mechanisms and their uncalibrated assumptions are documented in `simulation-assumptions.md`.

Physical/communication randomness uses BLAKE2b keyed by scenario seed, resident and event, so warning-dependent control flow cannot consume a shared draw stream. The initial-condition hash includes configuration, population attributes and the social graph. A counterfactual loads its baseline's saved configuration even after draft edits. Comparisons require matching initial conditions and simulation versions; Bedrock comparisons also require matching provider, model, region and decision protocol. The living-town upgrade uses engine version 1.4.0, with an explicit life decision protocol for the additional action. Existing recordings remain immutable and replayable.

New Bedrock generations are not promised deterministic, even with temperature zero. The saved decisions and resulting recordings are immutable replay evidence. Two warning variants share population and physical hazard; their model responses may differ.

An explicit `rules` mode retains the original deterministic response model. It is the schema default for backward-compatible API clients and old scenarios; the UI selects Bedrock for new work. Only in rules mode do notice/action/share probabilities and the preparation timer drive response. Changing wording alone in rules mode leaves those assumptions and resulting behavior unchanged. In Bedrock mode the actual received wording is interpreted by the model and can affect its choices; no measured human-response accuracy is claimed.

Every frame stores `[resident_id, x, y, state_index, cumulative_exposure_seconds]`, metrics, cumulative `hazard_cells`, current `active_hazard_cells`, `blocked_roads`, hazard type/center/wind/radius and latest available model decisions. Events distinguish delivery, notice, sharing, evacuation starts, shelter arrivals, exposure and replanning.

## Single simulator workspace

React, TypeScript and Vite provide one simulator workspace: select a disaster and warning, edit the message, run it and inspect results. Advanced opens focused drawers for scenario settings, warning settings, Mini/import controls, saved experiments and methods. Comparison views appear within the simulator rather than in separate application tabs.

PixiJS 8/WebGL renders an original top-down pixel city from a locally authored PNG atlas. The real 40×40 logic grid maps to four 16-pixel art tiles along each cell edge. Static roads, riverbanks, buildings, gardens and street furniture are baked once into a shared texture; resident sprites and hazard layers consume authoritative saved frames. One canvas/renderer, shared art textures, one camera and one replay clock support either a single world or two clipped comparison views. Pan, discrete zoom, keyboard camera controls, follow and overlapping-resident selection work inside the game workspace.

New recording format 2 adds actual per-tick traversed road vertices and normalized presentation fractions, plus sparse route changes. Smooth replay follows only validated recorded edges; old recordings remain readable and ambiguous legacy movement snaps. Exposure and hazards keep their existing semantics. Flames follow active cells, scorch follows affected cells, and tornado position follows its recorded center. Pause and reduced motion stop the animation ticker. Rendering never calls Bedrock or changes positions/outcomes.

Semantic React controls use pixel borders, original icons and the locally bundled OFL Pixelify Sans display font. Warning editing, history, outcomes, real Mini charts/imports and methods are contextual panels over the same world. Recharts supplies readable numerical charts. Optional native Web Audio ambience starts only after a user action and pauses with replay.

Warning runs use durable background jobs with immutable request snapshots, persisted idempotency keys, actual tick/round/batch progress and cooperative cancellation. A refresh reconnects to the same job. Worker advisory leases identify interrupted work without automatically sending another paid request; uncertain provider reservations remain accounted. See [recording and job contracts](recording-and-jobs.md).

## Independent local TRIBE v2 Mini

`LocalTribe` launches `backend/scripts/run_tribe_local.py` as an independent one-shot process. FastAPI does not import Torch or the TRIBE runtime. A worker lock prevents overlapping local jobs and a ten-minute subprocess timeout stops an overlong job. On this Mac, the pinned official Mini brain encoder runs on Apple Metal/MPS; pinned Llama 3.2 1B text features run on CPU. CPU brain execution is available when Metal is absent. Bedrock does not execute TRIBE.

The local experiment accepts UTF-8 text up to 16 KB and 200 words, presented at a defined 2.5 words/second. These are specified reading times, not measured speech timing. The official model outputs numerical time × 20,484 cortical-vertex predictions with pinned model/source revisions and recorded presentation protocol.

`POST /api/neural/analyze-warning` registers the exact saved warning and invokes the worker when needed. Reuse requires matching warning identity, both exact warning/stimulus hashes, official Mini model/source revisions, reading protocol/rate, real provenance and an existing NPZ. Text edits invalidate reuse. A linked run analysis is checked even at zero coupling: identity, both hashes, provenance and artifact presence must match.

Mini predictions accompany the resident simulation independently by default; Bedrock receives resident context and warning text rather than an invented neuroscience-to-behavior score. Optional neural notice-probability coupling is restricted to rules mode and is disabled by default. It adds `strength × tanh(mean(abs(predictions)))` to notice probability as an explicit, unvalidated sensitivity assumption.

Advanced retains stimulus and numerical artifact imports. Upload limits, ZIP entries and NPY headers are validated before allocation. Pickle/object arrays, unsafe shapes, nonfinite values and inconsistent time axes are rejected. Imported `REAL_TRIBE` metadata declares immutable origin revisions but does not cryptographically attest execution; test fixtures cannot drive coupling. Local jobs record that the app's official Mini worker computed them.
