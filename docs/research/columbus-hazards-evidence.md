# Columbus / Ohio State: hazard evidence and simulation design

Research checked **24 October 2026**. Scope: research and implementation recommendations; no application changes, cloud calls, or fitted behavioral parameters. Public sources establish definitions and protective actions. They do **not** validate this simulator's individual decisions, injuries, evacuation times, or building safety.

## Recommendation

Add four distinct experiences: **Blizzard**, **Hailstorm**, **Destructive thunderstorm**, and **Major Public Safety Threat**. First add hazard-specific sheltering, travel interruption, real simulation time, and explicit indoor occupancy. A storm that sends every resident across campus to a distant evacuation marker would teach the wrong behavior. A blizzard also cannot be represented honestly by a ten-minute event without an explicitly compressed, multi-hour scenario clock.

Franklin County identifies severe winter weather, severe summer weather, flooding, tornadoes, and extreme cold among its planning hazards. This supports local relevance, not a numerical probability of an event striking a particular campus building. [Franklin County THIRA](https://www.franklincountyohio.gov/Agency-Directory/Emergency-Management-and-Homeland-Security/Planning/Threat-Hazard-Identification-Risk-Assessment-THIRA)

## Source-to-mechanics table

The mechanics below are **proposed game design**, except the explicitly identified official criteria and guidance.

| Experience | Verified evidence | Recommended mechanics | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blizzard | WFO Wilmington requires sustained winds or frequent gusts **≥35 mph**, falling and/or blowing snow, and visibility **≤¼ mile for ≥3 hours**. [Local winter criteria](https://www.weather.gov/iln/criteria-winter) | Separate wind, visibility, snow cover, road condition, temperature, and elapsed qualifying conditions. Cancel discretionary trips; show occupants waiting indoors, drivers postponing departures, and a gradual weather recovery. | Snow depth is not the blizzard definition. Snow can blow without new snowfall. Extreme cold is a separate hazard. The local criteria use an inclusive quarter-mile boundary; some national summaries use “less than.” Preserve the chosen local definition and source version. |
| Hailstorm | Severe-thunderstorm base criteria include **≥1-inch hail**; hail is part of the severe-thunderstorm warning framework. [NWS damage-threat categories](https://www.weather.gov/lot/SevereThunderstormWarningsUpdate) | A moving hail footprint, diameter in inches/mm, audible/visible pellets, people taking the nearest eligible indoor shelter, and vehicle/building disruption represented separately. | Do not invent an official “Hailstorm Warning” product. No measured personal-injury or vehicle-damage curve has been established in this research. A few reported hail points do not establish continuous coverage. |
| Destructive thunderstorm | Base: **≥58 mph wind or ≥1-inch hail**. Considerable: **≥70 mph or ≥1.75-inch hail**. Destructive: **≥80 mph or ≥2.75-inch hail**. [NWS categories](https://www.weather.gov/lot/SevereThunderstormWarningsUpdate) | Use the destructive label for the requested very severe preset. Wind, rain, hail, lightning, fallen branches, and power disruption are distinct layers. Shift people into sturdy nearby buildings; interrupt outdoor gatherings and classes in transit. | These are warning damage-threat thresholds, not damage or casualty guarantees. Lightning may be dangerous without severe-warning criteria. Do not equate a warning polygon with uniform observed intensity. |
| Lightning within thunderstorms | NWS recommends a substantial building or enclosed metal-topped vehicle; stay sheltered **at least 30 minutes after the last thunder**. [NWS lightning tips](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-tips) | A resettable last-thunder timer; people stay indoors through an apparent lull. Shelter eligibility distinguishes a substantial building from a tent, open pavilion, or tree. | A vehicle can provide lightning shelter without being safe from tornadoes, floodwater, or all hail/wind effects. Do not make umbrellas or tree canopies protective objects. |
| Major Public Safety Threat | OSU describes multimodal alerts, including initially general messages followed by more detail. [OSU notices and alerts](https://dps.osu.edu/alert-notices) | A **fictional, officially designated dangerous area** with an avoidance perimeter, versioned generic instructions, route closures, staged updates, and an explicit all-clear. Agents follow the current instruction: avoid area, remain indoors, hold travel, or leave a specified area via available safe routes. | All incident timing, geometry, severity, and behavior are authored assumptions. No attacker entities, weapons, real incident recreation, tactics, security weaknesses, or crime-derived campus risk scores. Do not use the model to invent a real official alert. |

National winter guidance prioritizes avoiding travel during blizzard conditions. Campus severe-storm guidance tells people outdoors to seek a sturdy building and avoid windows. This supports **stay sheltered / seek shelter / postpone travel**, rather than universal evacuation. [NWS winter safety](https://www.weather.gov/safety/winter-snow), [OSU severe storm guidance](https://dps.osu.edu/severe-storm)

For winter presets, do not transplant a single national snowfall threshold. Wilmington publishes geographically varying winter-storm snow/sleet thresholds on its criteria map; this research did not resolve the map's exact Franklin County band. The blizzard definition above is independently explicit. A snow squall is a shorter, intense visibility event and should not be mislabeled a three-hour blizzard. [Wilmington criteria](https://www.weather.gov/iln/criteria-winter)

## Preserve correct distinctions in the existing hazards

| Existing hazard | Correct conceptual action | Required change or constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Tornado | Seek appropriate sturdy indoor shelter; OSU recommends an interior sturdy room on the lowest possible floor, away from windows. [OSU shelter guidance](https://dps.osu.edu/shelter-place) | A generic exterior refuge node, open field, car, or large open-span room must not count as a tornado shelter. Avoid driving residents across a hazard to an arbitrary map exit. [NWS tornado guidance](https://www.weather.gov/safety/tornado-during) |
| Flood | Follow evacuation directions where issued; never walk or drive through flooded roads. [CDC flood preparation](https://www.cdc.gov/floods/safety/index.html) | Disable flooded graph edges for both modes. Seek a reachable appropriate destination, not an unconstrained shortest path through water. Do not reuse the tornado basement rule where flooding makes a low floor unsuitable. |
| Building fire | Leave the affected building and alert others when safe; do not allow fire to cut off the exit. [OSU building-fire guidance](https://dps.osu.edu/building-fire-explosion) | Indoor occupancy is not universally protective. Distinguish building fire from an abstract spreading outdoor fire; do not claim a stylized fire-spread model predicts actual campus building propagation. |

FEMA/Ready.gov material was searched, but direct access to its floods page and preparedness PDF returned HTTP 403 during this review. No unverified FEMA-specific numerical claims are used. Accessible primary NWS, OSU, county, NCEI, and CDC material supports the recommendations above.

## Local historical anchors without false precision

**29 June 2012 derecho.** NWS Wilmington provides an event narrative and linked reports for the regional damaging-wind episode. It is a useful historical case for a fast-moving windstorm and widespread disruption. A campus playthrough can be “inspired by the 2012 Ohio derecho”; it must not be called an exact OSU reconstruction without spatially resolved observations. Do not transfer the largest regional gust to every campus cell. [NWS event archive](https://www.weather.gov/iln/20120629)

**23 December 2022 winter storm.** Wilmington's report includes a **4.8-inch snowfall report at KCMH at 7:00 a.m.** and reports ranging from 2.0 to 6.0 inches elsewhere in Franklin County. The page explicitly describes its gathered observations as unofficial. This is useful evidence of spatial variation and report provenance. Those amounts alone do not prove the blizzard wind/visibility/duration criteria held at OSU. Retrieve simultaneous station observations before asserting that. [NWS winter-event report](https://www.weather.gov/iln/20221223)

The regional archive is a curated set of significant cases, not a complete frequency denominator. [NWS Wilmington event archive](https://www.weather.gov/iln/events)

## Proposed architecture and honest parameter provenance

Separate four systems:

1. **Environment:** actual simulation seconds, meters, wind, visibility, precipitation, road accessibility, indoor eligibility, and shelter occupancy. Rendering time and playback speed must not change the modeled outcome. Weather intensity must not be a direct alias for an “exposed” status.
2. **Information:** each resident's delivered messages, visible cues, remembered advice, and latest alert version. They cannot automatically know the hidden future hazard field.
3. **Decision:** bounded Bedrock choices at meaningful events, checked against legal actions. An LLM chooses intentions; deterministic movement, occupancy, vehicle transitions, resource capacity, and action validation enforce the world. Routine locomotion does not require a paid call every frame.
4. **Presentation:** snow, hail, wind, resident gestures, indoor portraits, car boarding, family status, closures, and readable overlays. This layer expresses state; it must not fabricate events absent from the simulation.

Add semantic actions such as `SEEK_INDOOR_SHELTER`, `SHELTER_IN_PLACE`, `POSTPONE_TRAVEL`, `REROUTE`, and `EVACUATE_AREA`, plus an explicit authority-issued `ALL_CLEAR` event. Keep “waiting safely” distinct from “failed to move.” Weather may evolve without a new LLM decision on every tick. Generic public-safety all-clear is an authored scenario event, never an inferred response time or model-generated official assurance.

For families, preserve contact and reunification intentions without letting a dependent fetch pull an adult into a closed zone. Model safe contact/holding and later reunification. Cars require ownership or access, a parking location, boarding, seat capacity, a legal vehicle graph, and parking/alighting. Pedestrians and cars should not teleport between modes. Shelter capacities and accessibility can start as deliberately labeled authored values; no source above certifies actual capacity, opening hours, or designated shelter status for campus landmarks.

Use three provenance labels in scenario details:

| Label | Examples | What the user can conclude |
|---|---|---|
| **Observed** | Timestamped station gust, reported hail diameter, sourced historical snowfall | A specific source reported a value at its location and time, subject to its quality limits. |
| **Derived** | Unit conversion, interpolated weather curve, graph length from map geometry | The value follows a documented transformation; interpolation is still uncertain. |
| **Authored** | Alert receipt probability, trust, delay, road-speed reduction, household coordination, outage probability, shelter capacity, incident perimeter | A controllable educational assumption. It has not been measured for OSU residents. |

Do not invent local compliance coefficients from aggregate weather records. Do not label an arbitrary “seconds exposed” counter as a probability of injury or death. TRIBE Mini outputs are a separate neural-model artifact; they are not measurements of a resident's fear, alert compliance, or physiological harm. Comparisons between warning messages are conditional on the model assumptions, not causal evidence of real-world effectiveness.

## Validation and calibration gates

1. **Time and thresholds:** test each inclusive threshold, just-below boundaries, and the compound blizzard duration. A single gust is not proof of frequent gusts for three hours. Specify how intermittent conditions and missing observations are handled; report insufficient evidence rather than filling a gap with certainty. Test the lightning timer resetting on later thunder.
2. **Protection semantics:** an indoor storm-sheltered resident must differ from a person at the exterior doorway. The same room must not automatically be safe during a fire or flood. No family, accessibility fallback, or cached path may bypass a closure. Unreachable shelter produces a visible unmet need, not teleportation.
3. **Conservation:** people, household membership, vehicle seats, indoor occupants, and arrivals stay consistent. Pause, resume, playback speed, save/reload, and fixed-seed replay preserve outcomes.
4. **Historical reconstruction:** select several independently documented episodes; reserve whole episodes for holdout. Fit weather timing/magnitude to observed station/report data before fitting any behavioral assumptions. Do not randomly split reports from the same storm across training and validation. Compare observed and simulated curves with units, missing-data flags, and station/campus separation.
5. **Sensitivity:** vary authored alert delays, travel reductions, shelter availability, household coordination, and LLM decisions across seeds. Present a distribution or range of outcomes. If rankings between warning messages reverse under plausible assumptions, state that rather than declaring a winner.
6. **Learning and usability:** test whether players understand why staying indoors can be the successful action. Show sheltered, stranded, interrupted trips, blocked routes, and unmet needs; avoid a universal “evacuated = success” score. Keep the people visible against precipitation and provide reduced-motion/weather effects.
7. **Regression and costs:** replay old flood/fire/tornado examples and both warning conditions after action-schema changes. Version saved scenarios and the model adapter. Mock inference for the main test suite; make only bounded explicitly budgeted Bedrock checks. This research spent no AWS credits.

## Implementation order

First correct time units, indoor state, action validation, and hazard-specific destinations. Then build an accessible campus walking/driving graph and vehicle/household state transitions. Add one carefully tested severe-thunderstorm scenario, including the lightning hold timer, before layering hail and winter visibility. Add the abstract public-safety scenario as an instructional closure/alert exercise. Finally ingest pinned historical fixtures, expose parameter provenance in Advanced, and calibrate with clearly reported uncertainty.

The result can be locally grounded and educationally credible. It should not claim to forecast real OSU emergencies or reproduce measured individual behavior until separate validation supplies that evidence.
