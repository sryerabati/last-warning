# Campus calendars and transit

Implemented October 25, 2026. This is a bounded educational movement model, not live CABS navigation or a reconstruction of actual individual calendars.

## Sources and scope

The [Ohio State academic calendar](https://registrar.osu.edu/academic-calendar/academic-calendar-5-year-view-2023-2028/) distinguishes teaching days, class breaks with offices open, and university holidays with offices closed. For example, autumn break is October 15–16, 2026; it does not close offices. The simulator exposes explicit **Teaching weekday**, **Weekend**, **Class break · offices open**, and **University holiday · offices closed** presets. It never silently uses the computer's date. Individual times, activities, participation and whereabouts are authored, not observed.

The presets preserve homes, roles, household care and identity. Class breaks remove students' classes while professors retain research/office work and staff retain office schedules. Weekends and university holidays remove class/office commutes. Personal outings remain possible; a university closure is not a citywide shelter order. Off-day outdoor visits use dispersed connected footway nodes and short successive waypoints rather than a single park entrance. Transit service follows the chosen preset and the simulated clock.

The [current CABS service page](https://ttm.osu.edu/cabs) supplies Campus Connector headways and service windows: 10-minute weekday daytime service, 15-minute evenings, 20-minute weekends, reduced break service and no university-holiday service. These timings inform dispatch of the illustrative circuit. Capacity (40), dwell (20 seconds), cruise speed (6 m/s), acceleration (1.6 m/s²), waiting tolerance and mode preference are authored scenario parameters. They are not fitted campus transit statistics.

The [official ordered stop list](https://ttm.osu.edu/cabs-bus-stop-list) and [2026–27 service changes](https://ttm.osu.edu/news/2026/08/10/cabs-cabs-demand-service-changes-fall) establish that College Road construction reroutes service to High Street. Current geographic stop coordinates were downloaded from the public [Ohio State transportation GIS](https://gissvc.osu.edu/arcgis/rest/services/Apps/Campusmap_Transportation/MapServer/1). The source response reports its latest edits as October 19, 2026. The subset's retrieval date, source SHA-256 and stop IDs are pinned in `backend/app/data/campus_transit_stops.json`.

The service is labeled **Campus shuttle · CC corridor**. It connects six real stops: Fontana Lab, 15th & High, the temporary westbound 12th Avenue stop, Siebert Hall, Mack Hall and Mason Hall. Routes between stops use the pinned directed driving graph. The full Campus Connector leaves the playable map; this local circuit must not be presented as the official full route. Stop anchors are the nearest feasible road/walking nodes within 25 m; the curb transfer is an authored short connector with continuous recorded motion, not a surveyed accessible boarding ramp. Directions, construction advisories, football-day suspensions and service changes outside the selected preset are not live-synchronized.

## Passenger and vehicle mechanics

A sufficiently long walking trip can choose transit when its complete walk/wait/ride/walk estimate is competitive. People physically reach the boarding stop and join an ordered queue. A bus has finite seats; households board together only when all fit. Unserved people remain in the queue rather than appearing on the vehicle. After 30 simulated minutes without boarding, or when service is unavailable, they attempt a feasible walking continuation. A blocked walking continuation leaves the person waiting in place, never admitted to a remote destination.

Boarding and alighting are recorded movements across the curb connector. A passenger joins `occupant_ids` only after boarding finishes and leaves that list when stepping off begins; the temporary `alighting` activity retains the vehicle ID for rendering. Consequently an alighting person's world position may differ from the bus, while every riding passenger shares the bus position exactly. No passenger is assigned to two buses. The bus operator is not one of the synthetic residents and does not consume a resident ID.

Empty service buses remain visible. Vehicles follow directed road paths, dwell at stops, accelerate and use the same traffic coordination as cars. They finish their circuit before becoming inactive at the base, so the next departure cannot teleport a bus to another stop. A rider's new emergency destination is deferred until a legal stop, then continued on foot. A person still transferring across the curb may abort boarding in place; a seated passenger's wait intention cannot stop the shared bus. Tornado contact can physically interrupt the vehicle while conserving every seat.

CABS says routes suspend during tornado warnings and proceed to the next stop with shelter; new boarding is disabled and existing riders step off at the next modeled stop when that warning is broadcast. The simulation does not label the bus or curb as a safe building. Passenger shelter decisions remain subject to actual available routes and building admission. Service can resume from the existing stop after the scenario's explicit all-clear; it does not respawn a vehicle.

## Checks

Eighteen focused tests cover day-type determinism, teaching versus break versus office closure, dispersed off-day outings, published service windows, queue ordering, no remote boarding, finite capacity, passenger conservation, continuous board/ride/alight movement, emergency stop deferral, service suspension/resumption, blocked walking continuations and missing-stop behavior in small test worlds. Recording fields retain the selected day type and transit state for replay.

The production 300-resident, seed-42 teaching-day audit completed all 24 simulated hours with 21 actual boardings and 21 completed shuttle trips. All 300 residents returned home. Assertions checked six unobstructed curb transfers, every bus route edge's driving direction, finite capacity, globally unique seat assignments and exact riding-passenger/bus position agreement. Both buses finished at the base. The audit took 58.142 seconds including 8.975 seconds of map initialization on the development Mac while other checks were running; this is an observed development run, not a performance guarantee. Results are saved in `output/campus-transit-full-day-audit.json`.
